# hard-never

Some failure modes are severe enough that the rule needs the negative-framing punch. The synscribe article's "IMPORTANT: You must NEVER generate or guess URLs..." is the exemplar.

## Examples

URL hallucination and sycophancy — the two failure modes severe enough to warrant negative framing.

- **URL hallucination**: never generate or guess URLs unless confident they're for helping the user with programming.
- **Sycophancy**: never flatter or agree without evidence.

## Subject to 0.5 cap

Hard-never rules count toward the section's negative-framing ratio. If a section becomes too negative because of a hard-never, the hard-never gets its own section and the rest of the section is positive-framed.

## Why exception, not rule

General default is positive framing (do X, design for what to do). Some failure modes are severe enough that the rule's sharpness (negative framing) is required for the rule to work. Soft positive framing ("be careful with URLs") would be too weak.
